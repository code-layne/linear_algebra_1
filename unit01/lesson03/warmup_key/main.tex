\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
\small These Lesson~1.1 moves become today's \emph{matrix} and \emph{column space}. Answer quickly.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{1. Linear combination.} Scale each vector, then add.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2.0em, itemsep=10pt]
  \item $2\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + 3\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
        = {}$\ans{$\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\4 \end{bsmallmatrix}+\begin{bsmallmatrix} 0\\3 \end{bsmallmatrix}=\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\7 \end{bsmallmatrix}$}
  \item $1\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + 2\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}
        = {}$\ans{$\begin{bsmallmatrix} 3\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}+\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\4 \end{bsmallmatrix}=\begin{bsmallmatrix} 5\\5 \end{bsmallmatrix}$}
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{2. Is it a multiple?} Is $\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$ a multiple of
$\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$? Is $\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$?
\ansline{$\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\4 \end{bsmallmatrix}=2\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}$ --- yes, a multiple (parallel). $\begin{bsmallmatrix} 3\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}$ is \emph{not} a multiple of $\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}$ (not parallel).}

\vspace{6pt}
\textbf{3. Line or plane?} Two \emph{non-parallel} vectors in the plane --- do all their
combinations $c\vv+d\ww$ fill a single \emph{line} or the \emph{whole plane}?
\ansline{The whole plane --- non-parallel vectors reach every point.}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
Every item previews today's matrix. Item~1 \emph{is} $A\xx$: a linear combination of two columns
(1b is exactly $A\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}$ for $A=\begin{bsmallmatrix} 3&1\\1&2 \end{bsmallmatrix}$).
Item~2 is the parallel test that decides whether a column space is a line. Item~3 is the ``line
vs.\ plane'' fact that becomes the shape of the column space.
\end{teachernote}
