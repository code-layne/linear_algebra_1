\begin{remindbox}
\small Every $m\times n$ matrix $A$ of rank $r$ has \textbf{four fundamental subspaces}. Two live in the
\emph{input room} $\mathbb{R}^n$: the \textbf{row space} $C(A^{\mathsf T})$ (dimension $r$) and the
\textbf{nullspace} $N(A)$ (dimension $n-r$). Two live in the \emph{output room} $\mathbb{R}^m$: the
\textbf{column space} $C(A)$ (dimension $r$) and the \textbf{left nullspace} $N(A^{\mathsf T})$ (dimension
$m-r$). The single number $r$ fixes all four dimensions, and ``\emph{row rank $=$ column rank}'' says the
row space and column space always share it. One reduction of $A$ hands you a basis for each.
\end{remindbox}
