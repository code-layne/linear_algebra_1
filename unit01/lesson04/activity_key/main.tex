\begin{headlinebox}{blush}
\small\textbf{Rebuilding a Catalog from Base Products.} A workshop's catalog is a matrix $A$. Each
column is a product listed as $\begin{bsmallmatrix} \text{parts} \\ \text{labor} \end{bsmallmatrix}$.
Some products are \emph{bundles} of others, so we can keep just the \textbf{base} products in $C$ and
record a \textbf{recipe} $R$ that rebuilds the whole catalog: $A=CR$. Work with your partner and
\emph{show your steps}.
\end{headlinebox}

\vspace{0.10in}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40, title=\textbf{Tier R --- Remediate},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=1.5mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, breakable]
Base products $C=\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$ (columns $\mathbf{c}_1=\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}$,
$\mathbf{c}_2=\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\3 \end{bsmallmatrix}$) and recipe $R=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item Multiply \emph{by columns}: compute $C\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\0 \end{bsmallmatrix}$ and
        $C\begin{bsmallmatrix} 0\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}$ (the first two columns of the catalog).
\begin{work}
  1\,\mathbf{c}_1 + 0\,\mathbf{c}_2 &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
  0\,\mathbf{c}_1 + 1\,\mathbf{c}_2 &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\3 \end{bsmallmatrix}
\end{work}
  \item Compute the third catalog product $C\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}=1\,\mathbf{c}_1+2\,\mathbf{c}_2$.
\begin{work}
  1\,\mathbf{c}_1 + 2\,\mathbf{c}_2
      &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix} + \begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\6 \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
      &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 4\\7 \end{bsmallmatrix}
\end{work}
  \item Write out the full catalog $A=CR$. What does column~3 mean --- how is that product built
        from the base products?
\begin{work}
  CR &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 2 & 1 & 4\\1 & 3 & 7 \end{bsmallmatrix}
\end{work}
        \ansline{Product~3 is $1$ of base~1 plus $2$ of base~2 --- a bundle using $4$ parts and $7$ labor.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40, title=\textbf{Tier A --- Approaching Proficiency},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=1.5mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, breakable]
A catalog $A=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 1 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ (three products).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item Are columns~1 and~2 independent (not multiples)? These are the \textbf{base products} ---
        write $C$.
\begin{work}
  \begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}
      &\ne t\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix} \ \text{for any } t \\
  C &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 1 & 2\\2 & 1 \end{bsmallmatrix}
\end{work}
  \item Show product~3 is a bundle: find weights so $\begin{bsmallmatrix} 4\\5 \end{bsmallmatrix}
        = {\color{keyred}\mathbf{2}}\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix} + {\color{keyred}\mathbf{1}}\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}$.
        Use them to write $R$.
\begin{work}
  2\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix} + 1\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}
      &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 4\\5 \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
  R &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2\\0 & 1 & 1 \end{bsmallmatrix}
\end{work}
  \item \textbf{Interpret.} State $A=CR$ and the \textbf{rank}. What does the rank say about how many
        \emph{truly different} products this catalog really has?
\begin{work}
  A &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 1 & 2\\2 & 1 \end{bsmallmatrix}\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2\\0 & 1 & 1 \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
  \operatorname{rank}(A) &= 2
\end{work}
        \ansline{Only two products are truly different (the base products); the third is a bundle of those two.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40, title=\textbf{Tier E --- Extension},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=1.5mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, breakable]
A different catalog has every product on \emph{one line}:
$M=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em, itemsep=8pt]
  \item Show all three columns are multiples of $\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}$. What
        is the \textbf{rank} of $M$?
\begin{work}
  \begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\4 \end{bsmallmatrix} &= 2\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
  \begin{bsmallmatrix} 3\\6 \end{bsmallmatrix} &= 3\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
  \operatorname{rank}(M) &= 1
\end{work}
        \ansline{Only one independent column, so the rank is $1$ --- every product sits on one line.}
  \item Write $M=CR$ with $C$ a single column and $R$ a single row.
\begin{work}
  C &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
  R &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
  M &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bsmallmatrix}
\end{work}
  \item \textbf{Justify.} Explain why \emph{every} product in this catalog must use twice as much
        labor as parts --- no matter which base you pick.
\begin{work}
  t\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix} &= \begin{bsmallmatrix} t\\2t \end{bsmallmatrix} \\
  \text{labor} &= 2t = 2\,(\text{parts})
\end{work}
        \ansline{The whole column space is that one line, so no product can break the $1\!:\!2$ ratio.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
