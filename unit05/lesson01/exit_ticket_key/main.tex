No notes. Show your reasoning.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=16pt]
  \item \textbf{Compute a determinant.} Expand along a row to find
        $\det\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.
        \ansline{$3\det\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2 & 1\\ 1 & 2\end{bsmallmatrix} - 1\det\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1 & 1\\ 0 & 2\end{bsmallmatrix} + 0 = 3(3) - 1(2) = 9-2 = 7$.}
  \item \textbf{Volume.} Find the \emph{volume} of the box whose columns are
        $\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$, $\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$,
        $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$.
        \emph{Hint:} expand along the row or column with the most zeros.
        \ansline{The matrix is $\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2 & 0 & 1\\ 1 & 3 & 2\\ 0 & 0 & 4\end{bsmallmatrix}$. Expand along the bottom row: $\det = 4\det\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2 & 0\\ 1 & 3\end{bsmallmatrix} = 4(6)=24$. Volume $=|24|=24$.}
  \item \textbf{Explain.} The three columns of a $3\times3$ matrix all lie in one plane. What is its
        determinant, and is the matrix invertible? Explain in one sentence \emph{why}.
        \ansline{$\det=0$ and the matrix is \emph{not} invertible: three coplanar columns span a flat box with zero volume, so the transformation collapses space and cannot be undone.}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teachernote}
Sort into ``got it / expansion slip / concept slip.'' Item~1 is a clean cofactor expansion --- watch
the middle sign ($-1\times$ its minor). Item~2 rewards choosing the zero-rich bottom row and reading
$|\det|$ as volume (watch for a dropped absolute value). Item~3 is the target justification: coplanar
columns $\Rightarrow$ flat box $\Rightarrow\det=0\Rightarrow$ no inverse. If many stumble on item~1,
open 5.2 with a 60-second expansion re-warm.
\end{teachernote}
