No notes. Show your reasoning.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=16pt]
  \item \textbf{Area factor.} For $A=\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$, compute $\det A$.
        What is the area of the image of the unit square? Of a triangle with area $3$?
        \ansline{$\det A=(4)(2)-(1)(0)=8$. Unit square $\to$ area $|\det A|=8$; a triangle of area $3\to 8\cdot3=24$.}
  \item \textbf{Orientation.} Which of these flips orientation, $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 2
        \end{bmatrix}$ or $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$? Give the area factor $|\det|$ of
        each and explain how the determinant tells you.
        \ansline{$\det\begin{bsmallmatrix} 3 & 0\\ 0 & 2\end{bsmallmatrix}=6>0$ (area factor $6$, orientation preserved). $\det\begin{bsmallmatrix} 0 & 2\\ 2 & 0\end{bsmallmatrix}=0-4=-4<0$ (area factor $4$, orientation \emph{flipped}). The \emph{second} flips orientation --- a negative determinant signals a reflection.}
  \item \textbf{Explain.} A matrix $A$ has $\det A=0$. What does its transformation do to the unit
        square, and why can the transformation \emph{not} be undone? \ansline{It collapses the unit square flat --- onto a line or a point (area $0$). Once the plane is squashed to a line, many different points land on the same image point, so there is no way to send each image back to a unique original --- $A$ has no inverse.}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{teachernote}
Sort into ``got it / forgot $|\cdot|$ / sign slip.'' Item~1 is the core payoff: area factor $=|\det A|$
applied to two regions. Item~2 separates \emph{size} (the area factor $|\det|$) from \emph{sign}
(orientation) --- the negative determinant is the tell. Item~3 is the target justification: $\det=0$
collapses the plane flat, so the map is not invertible (the Unit~2 ``singular'' seen geometrically). If
many miss item~1, open the Unit~5 review with a 60-second ``unit square $\to$ column parallelogram, area
$=|\det|$'' re-warm before the test.
\end{teachernote}
