\begin{learningtargetbox}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item \ldots{} factor a matrix as $A=LU$: $U$ is elimination's result, and $L$ \textbf{stores the multipliers} (with $1$s on its diagonal).
  \item \ldots{} \textbf{check} a factorization by multiplying $LU$ back to $A$.
  \item \ldots{} solve $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ in \textbf{two triangular sweeps} --- forward-solve $L\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{b}$, then back-solve $U\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{c}$.
  \item \ldots{} explain why $A=LU$ is the efficient way to re-solve one system for many right-hand sides.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small The factorization $A=LU$ \textbf{saves} elimination: $U$ is the upper-triangular result, and
$L$ is lower-triangular with $1$s on the diagonal and the \textbf{multipliers} $\ell$ below it --- no
extra work, since those are the same multipliers elimination already used. To solve
$A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$, take \textbf{two sweeps}: forward-solve $L\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{b}$ (top down),
then back-solve $U\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{c}$ (bottom up). Factor once, and every new $\mathbf{b}$ is just
two quick sweeps.
\end{remindbox}
