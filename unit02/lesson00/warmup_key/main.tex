\textbf{1. Solving a small system.} Find $x$ and $y$ that make \emph{both} true. Use any method.
\[
  x + y = 6, \qquad x - y = 2.
\]
\hspace{2em} $x = {}$\ans{$4$} \qquad $y = {}$\ans{$2$}
\begin{work}
  (x+y)+(x-y) &= 6+2 \\
           2x &= 8 \\
            x &= 4 \\
            y &= 6-4 = 2
\end{work}

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{2. $A\xx$ as a combination of columns.} With
$A=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\xx=\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$,
scale each column by the matching weight and add:
\[
  A\xx = 3\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} + 1\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}
       = \begin{bmatrix}{\color{keyred}\mathbf{5}}\\[2pt]{\color{keyred}\mathbf{11}}\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{3. Is the target reachable?} The matrix $B=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$
has columns that are parallel (the second is $3\times$ the first), so every $B\xx$ lands on one
line. Is $\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$ on that line --- that is, in the column space of $B$?
\ansline{Yes --- $\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\4 \end{bsmallmatrix}=2\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}$ is a multiple of the column direction, so it is on the line.}
