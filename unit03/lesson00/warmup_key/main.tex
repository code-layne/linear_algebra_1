\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Skills We'll Use Today}
\small These three skills from Unit~1 power everything in today's lesson. Answer quickly.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{1. A linear combination.} With $\vv=\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and
$\ww=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$, scale and add:
\[
  2\vv + 1\ww = 2\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + 1\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}
  = \begin{bmatrix}{\color{keyred}\mathbf{5}}\\[2pt]{\color{keyred}\mathbf{5}}\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{2. Same line, or not?} Two vectors span a \emph{line} if one is a multiple of the other,
and a whole \emph{plane} if they point in different directions. For each pair, circle
\textbf{line} or \textbf{plane}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item $\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$:
        \hfill \ans{line} \;($\begin{bsmallmatrix} 4\\2 \end{bsmallmatrix}=2\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}$) \qquad / \qquad plane
  \item $\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$:
        \hfill line \qquad / \qquad \ans{plane} \;(different directions)
\end{itemize}

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{3. Is the target reachable?} Is $\begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}$ a combination of
the columns of $A=\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$ --- that is, in the column space of
$A$? Try to find weights $x_1,x_2$ with
$x_1\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}+x_2\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\3 \end{bsmallmatrix}=\begin{bsmallmatrix} 5\\5 \end{bsmallmatrix}$.
\ansline{Yes --- $x_1=2,\ x_2=1$: $2\begin{bsmallmatrix} 2\\1 \end{bsmallmatrix}+1\begin{bsmallmatrix} 1\\3 \end{bsmallmatrix}=\begin{bsmallmatrix} 5\\5 \end{bsmallmatrix}$. The columns point in different directions, so \emph{every} target is reachable.}
\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
Item~2 previews ``line vs.\ plane through the origin''; item~3 is the reachability / column-space
idea we rename as ``subspace'' today. If a student says the answer to~3 is ``no,'' have them notice
the two columns are independent, so $C(A)=\mathbb{R}^2$ and \emph{any} $\mathbf b$ works.
\end{teachernote}
