# High School Linear Algebra Course Design Notes

## Core Decision

The course will be built around:

- **Primary Text:** *Linear Algebra for Everyone* — Gilbert Strang  
- **Supplementary Resources:**  
  - MIT OCW 18.06 (Strang lectures)  
  - *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang (selective use)

---

## Guiding Philosophy

This course prioritizes:

- **Conceptual understanding over formal abstraction**
- **Clarity and structure over completeness**
- **Applications as illustration, not as the central burden**

Students should leave the course understanding:

- What a matrix *does* (transformations)
- How systems of equations behave
- The meaning of span, independence, and basis
- Why eigenvectors matter
- How linear algebra applies to real-world systems

---

## Key Pedagogical Shift

### From:
> Linear Algebra + Mathematical Modeling (co-equal goals)

### To:
> Linear Algebra as the core subject, with **guided applications**

---

## Role of Modeling

Modeling is included in a **constrained, structured way**, not as an open-ended discipline.

### Instructor provides:
- Model structure
- Assumptions
- Setup

### Students provide:
- Computation
- Interpretation
- Connection to meaning

---

## Appropriate Applications for HS Level

### Include:
- Systems of equations in context (mixtures, networks)
- Matrix transformations (geometric interpretation)
- Least squares (line of best fit)
- Introductory Markov chains (state transitions)
- Eigenvectors as “natural directions”

### Scale Down:
- Open-ended modeling problems
- Multi-step real-world model construction
- Continuous systems and differential equations connections

### Exclude:
- Proof-heavy modeling frameworks
- Advanced optimization
- Tasks requiring simultaneous abstraction + creativity + technical setup

---

## Use of Supplementary Materials

### *Introduction to Linear Algebra*
Used for:
- Stronger problem sets
- More precise definitions
- Occasional deeper structure

Avoid:
- Full assignment of sections
- Early heavy abstraction

---

### MIT OCW 18.06

Instructor use:
- Extract clear explanations
- Use diagrams and visual intuition
- Adapt key insights into lessons

Student use:
- Selected clips or guided notes only

Avoid:
- Assigning full lecture sequences
- Overexposing students to abstraction

---

## Course Structure (Suggested Units)

### Unit 1: Systems of Equations as Geometry
- Linear systems
- Vectors and span
- Solutions as geometric objects

---

### Unit 2: Matrices as Transformations
- Matrix multiplication as composition
- Linear transformations of the plane
- Identity and inverse

---

### Unit 3: Independence, Basis, Dimension
- Linear independence
- Basis and coordinate systems
- Dimension as structure

---

### Unit 4: Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors
- Geometric meaning
- Stretching directions
- Connection to systems behavior

---

### Unit 5: Least Squares and Data
- Overdetermined systems
- Best-fit solutions
- Real-world interpretation

---

### Optional Unit: Discrete Systems
- Markov chains
- Long-term behavior
- Transition matrices

---

## Course Identity

Recommended titles:

- **Linear Algebra: Structure, Systems, and Applications**
- **Linear Algebra with Applications**

Avoid framing as a “Modeling” course.

---

## Instructional Principle

> A strong course is not defined by how much truth it contains,  
> but by how much truth students can successfully internalize.

---

## Outcome Goals

Students should:

- Build durable intuition for linear systems
- Understand matrices as operators, not just arrays
- Be prepared for college-level linear algebra
- Recognize linear algebra in real-world contexts

---

## Instructor Advantage

This structure allows:

- Differentiation (core vs. advanced problems)
- Use of deeper material without overwhelming students
- Alignment with both conceptual and rigorous mathematics

---

## Final Summary

- Use *Linear Algebra for Everyone* for understanding
- Use *Introduction to Linear Algebra* for depth (selectively)
- Use OCW 18.06 as an instructor resource
- Treat applications as guided, not open-ended
- Prioritize clarity, structure, and meaning

This results in a rigorous, appropriate, and highly effective high school linear algebra course.
