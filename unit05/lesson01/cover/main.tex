\begin{remindbox}
\small A $3\times3$ matrix carries \emph{one} number, its \textbf{determinant}, just as a $2\times2$
did. Compute it by \textbf{cofactor expansion} --- break it into three $2\times2$ determinants you
already know, with signs $+\,-\,+$. Geometrically, $|\det A|$ is the \emph{volume} of the box the
three columns span. When $\det A=0$ the three columns lie in one \emph{plane}, the box goes flat, and
the matrix has \emph{no inverse} --- the same story as Lesson 5.0, one dimension higher.
\end{remindbox}
