\begin{remindbox}
\small A \textbf{subspace} is a set of vectors you cannot escape by \textbf{adding} or
\textbf{scaling}: combine any members and you land back inside. Every subspace therefore contains
the zero vector $\mathbf{0}$, and looks like a flat piece pinned to the origin --- a line, a
plane, all of $\mathbb{R}^n$, or just $\{\mathbf{0}\}$. The \textbf{column space} $C(A)$ --- all
targets $A\mathbf{x}$ you can reach --- is the prime example.
\end{remindbox}
