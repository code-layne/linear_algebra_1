\begin{notesbox}{Getting Ready: Center, Spread, and a Symmetric Matrix}
\small Today we measure the \emph{shape} of data --- where it centers, how it spreads, and how features move
together. That rests on three familiar moves: an average, a sum of squared distances, and the eigenvalues of a
symmetric matrix. Warm up on each.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{1. An average (Unit 1).} Four students score $2,4,6,8$ on a quiz. Their \emph{mean} score is
\[
  \mu=\frac{2+4+6+8}{4}={\color{keyred}\mathbf{5}}.
\]
The mean is the center (balance point) of the data.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{2. A sum of squared distances (Unit 4).} Measure how far each score sits from the mean $\mu=5$, square
each distance, and add:
\[
  (2-5)^2+(4-5)^2+(6-5)^2+(8-5)^2=9+1+1+9={\color{keyred}\mathbf{20}}.
\]
Dividing this by $N=4$ will be our measure of \emph{spread}.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{3. Eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix (Unit 6).} Find the eigenvalues of
$V=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}5&3\\3&5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ by solving $\det(V-\lambda I)=0$:
\[
  (5-\lambda)^2-9=0 \;\Rightarrow\; \lambda={\color{keyred}\mathbf{8}}\ \text{or}\ {\color{keyred}\mathbf{2}}.
\]
\end{notesbox}

\begin{teachernote}
Each item is a piece of today's lesson. Item~1 is the \textbf{mean} $\mu=5$ (the center). Item~2 is the
numerator of the \textbf{variance}: the sum of squared distances from the mean is $20$, and $20/4=5$ will be the
spread $\sigma^2$. Item~3 is the punchline in advance: $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}5&3\\3&5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$
is the \textbf{covariance matrix} the notes build, and its eigenvalues $8,2$ are the variances along its
principal directions (Unit~7 PCA). $(5-\lambda)^2=9\Rightarrow 5-\lambda=\pm3\Rightarrow\lambda=2,8$. Debrief:
the whole lesson is ``center, spread, and the directions of spread'' --- an average, a squared distance, and a
symmetric matrix, all tools we already have.
\end{teachernote}
